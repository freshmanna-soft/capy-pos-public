The moment

A near-tie is a refusal today, on purpose

candidate-ranking.ts caps a top candidate below the 0.85 auto-add threshold whenever it lands within 0.05 of the runner-up — deliberately. Sorting a near-tie still produces a winner, and that winner is array order, which is to say chance. Refusing to guess is the correct call.

But refusing isn't the same as helping — and in this pilot there's no one to hand the refusal to. Capy Clerk runs self-checkout on each coworker's own phone, honor-system-adjacent, with nobody behind a counter. A near-tie just sits there: the yuzu holds at amber, nothing gets added, and the only person who can notice is whoever's holding the phone, mid-errand, with no cashier to flag it to. The proposal is narrow — when the two channels already wired into this till, a look and a listen, each have half an answer, let them finish each other's sentence instead of one of them staying silent.

Persona scenario

Maria, alone at the snack bar on her break

Maria doesn't run the till — nobody does. She just opens it on her own phone the way everyone here does, points the camera, and expects it to work without her having to think about it.

12:47 PM · no one else at the snack bar · a pastry, still in its wax bag, held up to her phone

The frame settles. FrameGate captures. Vision comes back with two names, eight hundredths of a point apart: Banana Bread, 0.54, and Coffee Cake, 0.52. Close enough that either is plausible, and close enough that picking one anyway would just be picking randomly with confidence.

TodayNothing happens. The yuzu hangs at amber and won't ripen. There's no cashier to catch it — just Maria, her phone, and an item that quietly isn't in her cart. She doesn't necessarily notice; she's on a break, not proofreading a receipt. She may just walk off with it, not dishonestly, just because the till never said anything was wrong.

ProposedThe capybara tilts and asks once — "Banana bread, or the coffee cake?" — with both names on her own screen in case her phone's mic is losing to the office. Maria: "Banana bread." rankLabelsBySpokenWords resolves it against the two candidates already on screen, not the whole catalogue. The ripple lands. She pays and heads back to her desk.

Same near-tie, twice. One version fails silently, with no one around to catch it. The other costs one sentence and closes the loop on the sale itself — not just the convenience of it.

Premortem — it shipped, it's been live a month, what broke

Eight ways this goes wrong before it goes right

High

Confidently wrong, with no second set of eyes

Speech recognition mishears "banana" as "vanilla" in a busy kitchen, and the till resolves against the wrong candidate with no visible doubt at all. In a staffed till this is annoying; self-checkout has no cashier, however inattentive, to catch it — the wrong charge lands on one specific coworker with no reconciliation step to surface it.

Design constraint: the resolved answer needs the same confidence bar the vision side already enforces. Reuse rankLabelsBySpokenWords's coverage tie-break rather than a bare string match, and fall back to the on-screen tap if the spoken answer doesn't clear it.
High

The mic doesn't know it's being asked a question

A disambiguation question and a general voice command ("add a sandwich") arrive on the same channel. Without a dedicated pending-answer state, an unrelated utterance in the answer window gets misread as the response.

Design constraint: needs its own intent state, the way checkout, mute, and mic-off already have one — not folded into the general keyword parser as one more case.
High

A phone is not a till, and the till's controls assume one

V/A/Q are keyboard shortcuts. There's no keyboard on a phone, and a pending question can be killed by things a fixed till never had to survive: the screen timing out, a text notification backgrounding the tab, the browser suspending getUserMedia to save battery — all mid-sentence, all silent.

Design constraint: the switches need touch affordances, not just relabeled shortcuts, and the pending-disambiguation state needs to survive (or cleanly cancel on) a background/foreground cycle — a phone backgrounding itself is the mobile equivalent of the watchTrackLoss race below, and it will happen far more often than a till operator hitting a hotkey mid-flow.
Medium

Asking too often defeats the point

If the 0.05 near-tie band is even slightly too generous for the real catalogue, disambiguation fires on ties an experienced eye would call obvious. There's no rush to speed through here — the cost is personal irritation, item by item, for whoever's just trying to grab a granola bar and get back to their desk. Annoy them enough and they stop trusting the self-checkout and start asking a coworker to "just spot them one," which is exactly the trust the honor system depends on.

Design constraint: RecognitionLogService already logs tier, confidence, and every correction. Measure the real near-tie rate before shipping — don't tune the threshold from a guess.
Medium

Every phone is a different camera and a different room

One fixed till means one lens, one mount, one ambient noise floor. Self-checkout on personal phones means recognition accuracy now varies by whoever's holding it — an old phone's camera, a hand that's shaking slightly, the kitchen's fridge hum versus a quiet hallway.

Design constraint: the near-tie rate is not one number any more — it should be watched per device class where the logging allows it, not assumed uniform from whatever hardware this was built and tested against.
Low

A second paid call hiding inside "just make it smarter"

The first ask for free-form phrasing or more than two candidates is reasonable. Routed through the agentic tier, every resolution quietly becomes a second model call stacked on the look that already happened.

Design constraint: ship the templated, keyword-only path as the default (see Cost, below) and treat an agentic upgrade as its own costed decision — not a silent one. (This is also exactly the gap a trained closed-set classifier — see the follow-on plan — is meant to close from the other direction.)
Medium

This project's proven blind spot: screenshot-shaped bugs

Every real capybara/HUD defect found so far — the frozen opening pose, the NaN-gradient crash, the camera that quietly turned itself back on — was caught by looking at the screen, not by a passing test suite. A joint voice-and-vision state machine, now running on mobile Safari and Chrome instead of one desktop build, is exactly this shape of bug again, with more browsers to be wrong on.

Design constraint: budget a manual look-and-listen pass through the actual disambiguation flow on real phones, not just unit coverage on the parser or a desktop e2e run.
Low

Nobody's watching the register any more, and that's bigger than disambiguation

This feature assumes the general shape of "no cashier" was already accepted for the pilot. Worth stating plainly rather than absorbing quietly: any till behavior that used to lean on "someone will notice" — an obviously-wrong total, a double-add, a stuck cart — inherits the same gap. Disambiguation is the sharpest edge of it, not the whole of it.

Design constraint: out of scope for this memo, but worth its own pass — what else in the checkout flow quietly assumed a human was standing there.
Value & cost — cloud services

The recommended path costs nothing extra to run

Why "cloud services" cost is mostly a non-question here. The recommended path adds no new Code Engine app, no new Terraform resource, and no new container — it's a client-side resolution against data capy-vision-proxy already returned. Even the agentic fallback only calls a service that's already live and already provisioned at scale_min_instances: 0. Tonight's decision to commit fully to IBM Code Engine over AWS (#221 / #224 / #225) means this sits on infrastructure already paid for by request, not a new line item on the estate.

One open question, not a blocker: if the agentic path is ever routed through IBM's own model gateway (ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL → api.servicesessentials.ibm.com, already wired for exactly this kind of routing decision), the effective per-token rate may differ from Anthropic's direct pricing above. Not confirmed this session — worth a real quote before committing spend to that path.

Sustainability — exec summary

Net effect on the carbon footprint: negligible, by design

Not negligible by luck — the recommended path was chosen so the common case adds zero model calls, and the fallback only reuses compute the estate already pays for whether or not it fires.

  • The recommended path adds no model call at all — a near-tie resolves from data the look already paid for, matched the same client-side way a spoken product name already is. Zero marginal compute, for the common case.
  • Even the agentic fallback reuses clerk-agent-relay, a service already scaled to zero between requests. IBM Code Engine's serverless model means this feature's footprint tracks actual use, not provisioned capacity — no new always-on server, no new idle draw.
  • Voice in and out both run client-side on hardware already powered on for the shift (the browser's own speech APIs) — strictly less compute than routing the same exchange through a cloud speech service.
  • For scale, not precision: independent research on LLM inference generally places a single query somewhere from a fraction of a gram to a few grams of CO₂e, depending on model size and the hardware behind it — not an Anthropic-specific figure, and not verified this session. At this feature's realistic volume (a minority of looks, not every look), even the high end of that range stays immaterial next to a shop's existing draw — lighting, refrigeration, the till itself running the full shift regardless.

If this ever needs to move from directional to defensible for an actual sustainability report, the right source is IBM's own Cloud Carbon Calculator run against the real Code Engine project — not an estimate layered on top of this memo.

Recommendation

Ship the templated path first. It costs nothing to run, touches no new infrastructure, and reuses a matcher (rankLabelsBySpokenWords) already proven in production for exactly this kind of voice-to-candidate resolution.

Before it ships: solve the mic-state conflict (Premortem #2) — that's the one risk here that isn't a tuning problem, it's a design problem. Instrument the real near-tie rate via RecognitionLogService before ever touching the 0.05 threshold, and don't let "make it smarter" quietly become the agentic tier without its own costed decision.
